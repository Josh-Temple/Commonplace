# Financial macro for trading — source overview

## Purpose

Define the cluster boundaries and the distinction between policy transmission, financial conditions, and trading interpretation.

## Evidence record

- **Monetary Policy: What Are Its Goals? How Does It Work?** — Federal Reserve Board, updated 2021. https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/monetary-policy-what-are-its-goals-how-does-it-work.htm — Supports the broad transmission from policy through market rates, asset prices, and economic activity. Limitation: an institutional overview, not a trading model.
- **The Federal Reserve’s Policy Tools** — Federal Reserve Board. https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/policytools.htm — Supports definitions of administered rates, open-market operations, discount window, and reserve-related tools. Limitation: operational description changes as frameworks evolve.
- **Global Financial Stability Report** — IMF, recurring publication. https://www.imf.org/en/Publications/GFSR — Supports the broad use of financial conditions, leverage, and market-functioning analysis. Limitation: periodic macro-financial assessment, not asset-specific causality.

## Confidence decision

Official definitions, documented procedures, and pricing mechanics support medium or higher confidence in definitions. Directional asset relationships, event reactions, lead/lag claims, and causal attribution remain conditional; added detail does not raise their confidence.

## Editorial limits

These sources support definitions and causal channels, not a stable trading coefficient or mechanical signal. Market reactions depend on expectations, starting conditions, risk premiums, positioning, and the shock's cause. URLs were selected from official or institutional publishers; no current-policy claim is preserved here.
