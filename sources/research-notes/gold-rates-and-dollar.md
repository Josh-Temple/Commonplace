# Gold, rates, and the dollar — research note

## Purpose

Bound the opportunity-cost, currency, safe-haven, and structural-demand channels without claiming permanent correlation.

## Evidence record

- **The Golden Dilemma** — Claude B. Erb and Campbell R. Harvey, *Financial Analysts Journal* 2013; working version SSRN 2078535. https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2078535 — Supports skepticism toward simple gold/inflation narratives. Limitation: historical sample and specification do not fix future relationships.
- **Gold Demand Trends** — World Gold Council, recurring industry research. https://www.gold.org/goldhub/research/gold-demand-trends — Supports categorized demand including investment, jewelry, technology, and official sector. Limitation: industry-funded research; methodology and revisions require review.
- **International Reserves and Foreign Currency Liquidity (IRFCL)** — IMF Data. https://data.imf.org/IRFCL — Supports official reserve reporting context. Limitation: reporting coverage/timing and valuation changes complicate flow inference.
- Federal Reserve TIPS and broad-dollar sources in the companion notes support real-yield and dollar definitions; they do not establish a stable gold beta.

## Confidence decision

Official definitions, documented procedures, and pricing mechanics support medium or higher confidence in definitions. Directional asset relationships, event reactions, lead/lag claims, and causal attribution remain conditional; added detail does not raise their confidence.

## Editorial limits

These sources support definitions and causal channels, not a stable trading coefficient or mechanical signal. Market reactions depend on expectations, starting conditions, risk premiums, positioning, and the shock's cause. URLs were selected from official or institutional publishers; no current-policy claim is preserved here.
